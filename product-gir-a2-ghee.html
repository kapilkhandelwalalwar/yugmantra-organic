The long version

The Gir is the cow our grandmothers kept — small, hardy, sacred, and stubbornly low-yielding. That low yield is the whole point. Her milk is A2 beta-casein, richer in fat globules and carotene, and it makes a ghee that is unmistakably yellow, unmistakably grainy, and unmistakably fragrant from across the room.

We collect the morning milk, set it into curd overnight with a live culture, hand-churn that curd in a clay pot at dawn, lift the butter off by hand, and simmer it over a slow flame until the water has gone and only the aroma is left. Twenty-five litres of milk become one litre of ghee. Nothing is added. Nothing is taken out.
